# Ostraka: Strategic Flora & Bestiary
### Compiled from the notes of Clary, First Archivist of the Order of the Clockwork

*Part of the Ostraka canon reference set. Eighth volume, covering the plants and creatures whose value goes beyond the merely biological — and the mid-canopy peoples whose lives are built around cultivating them.*

---

### A Note on Incomplete Records

I want to be honest about something before this volume begins, because I think a false completeness is worse than an admitted gap. Not every entry in this collection comes to me with the same depth of detail. Some plants I have walked among myself, watched harvested, understood down to the chemistry. Others survive in my archive as little more than a name, a subtitle, and the people who grow them — the fuller accounts lost somewhere between whoever first catalogued them and myself. I record what I know plainly, and where I know less, I say so rather than inventing the difference. A reader deserves to know which is which.

---

## I. The Botanical Codex

I want to begin with the two plants I understand most completely, because I think they illustrate something true about how danger and usefulness tend to travel together on this continent.

**Grave-Root**, called the Predator of the Ambush, is carnivorous flora clever enough to mimic stone or dead timber convincingly enough to fool most creatures that wander near it. It hunts through root-based vibration sensing, striking ground-level prey the moment they cross into range — and, tellingly, it avoids high-risk Flux-Shift zones entirely on its own, which tells me the plant has some instinct for chaos danger that most of the animals it preys on lack. **Stone-Root**, Petrified Lumber by its more common name, is a fossilized mineral-plant essential to heavy construction and to the furniture of firearms alike, its mineral density high enough that it resists fire outright. Between the two of them, I think you have a fair summary of how this continent's flora tends to work: everything useful here has also, at some point, killed something to earn that usefulness.

**Lith-Weave**, the Stone-Silk, took me considerably longer to understand than either of the above, and I record the connection here because I was genuinely pleased with myself the day I finally confirmed it. This is the actual material powering the Meridian Chain's Ghost-Light Towers — the lighthouses that project Anti-Chaos Fields, carving safe shipping lanes and shielding the Chain's Bridge-Cities from time-warps that would otherwise make trade impossible. A mineral-fiber silk, strong and stable enough to be woven directly into a tower's structure and used as a living conduit to hold an Anti-Chaos Field steady, managed by the Order of the Wick's own monastic Eels, who harvest the raw material from a Sacred Grove itself. I suspect, though I have not yet confirmed it to my full satisfaction, that Lith-Weave grows specifically in the high-chaos margins ringing a Grove, its fiber toughened by the same dangerous proximity that shapes Grave-Root and Stone-Root in their own environments.

**Crimson-Clove**, called Numb-Spice, I can confirm by its proper name — Syzygium Anestheticum, which tells you plainly enough what it does — cultivated by the Vulpines in the mid-canopy Scent-Gardens. Its numbing property is beyond doubt. What I have not been able to confirm, though I hold my own theory with some confidence, is its full application: I believe it is the medicinal agent Ink-Wrights use during the Shadow-Set ritual, since nothing else in any record I've recovered explains how a person survives that high-risk, surgical tattoo rite while remaining conscious enough to consent to its terms throughout. **Whisper-Berry**, Sparkle-Fruit by its subtitle, carries the scientific name Vitis Aeris — "air-vine" — and is cultivated by the Simians in the Wind-Tunnels. Here too I record my own reasoning honestly rather than dressing it up as settled fact: I believe it is chaos-sensitive, glimmering more intensely the closer a flow-shift draws, doubling as both a natural early warning for those who can't afford a Jitter-Rig and a sharpening aid for a Whisperer's own empathic craft.

Five further entries survive in my archive as little more than a name and the people who tend them, and I record them here exactly that plainly, because I would rather leave a gap honest than fill it with invention. Ghost-Teak, called Float-Wood, grown by the Sciurini in the dead, petrified trees of the Crown-Layer. Grave-Berry, the Memory-Fruit, tended by the Eastern Hounds among their own Bone-Fields — a detail I find fitting, given everything I've written elsewhere about that people's fraught relationship with memory itself. Shadow-Truffle, called Fungal Chocolate, cultivated by the Simians in the deep, hidden Root-Systems beneath the canopy floor. Silver-Needle Tea, or Ice-Blood, grown by the Red Pandas at the high, cold Cloud-Peaks. And Sun-Melon, Nature's own Canteen, grown by the Simians again, this time in the full light of the Upper Canopy.

## II. Ghost Flowers

I have saved the most consequential plant in this entire chapter for its own section, because I do not think a single line in a table does it justice.

Ghost Flowers — properly, Lunar Ghost-Blooms — are biological chaos-sponges, absorbing the ambient Moon-Haze that drifts across a region rather than any concentrated flow, and they simply do not stop growing, capping only once they reach roughly three meters in height. Older growth runs denser, and denser growth is worth considerably more to anyone willing to risk harvesting it. I want to record a warning here plainly, because I have seen the consequence of ignoring it firsthand: burning a field of Ghost-Blooms does not merely produce smoke. It releases a mutation cloud, and I do not recommend being anywhere nearby when that happens.

They are harvested, at real and ongoing personal risk, by the Dust-Husk Riders, and I have documented three separate industries built entirely on what they bring back. Aether-Clear, the Quill-Kin's genuine medicinal export, I have already written of at length elsewhere in this collection — I record here only that its source is now fully confirmed. Void-Cream serves the continent's cosmetic trade. And the 4-Year Seed, the third and darkest of the three, is a narcotic I have chosen not to describe in further detail in a document meant for general reference. Every essential Aether-engine on Ostraka depends, somewhere in its supply chain, on this single flower — smuggled through the Sump-Kin's Silent Current and the Heartland Alliance's River Ghosts alike, carried always inside the water-rich cavities of a Cactus-Kin, since exposure to open air causes what harvesters call Dry Rot, petrifying the bloom into worthless stone within hours.

## III. The Bestiary

I want to note, before I begin this section properly, that the creatures of Ostraka resist tidy narrative treatment more than almost anything else in this collection — most of what survives in my archive about them is precise and useful without being especially storied, the kind of practical knowledge a rancher or a soldier needs rather than the kind a historian lingers over. I record them here as I found them, without padding the account past what the evidence actually supports.

The **Ash-Bison**, called Grey-Back or the Living Rock, remains the primary food, leather, and bone source across much of the continent's plains, migratory in the way any sensible herd animal has to be on a world this unpredictable. The **Cloud-Cutter Ray** — Cutters, Sky-Dolphins, Storm-Stingers depending on who you ask — carries a ramming crest and a paralytic sting, an active chameleon skin that makes it genuinely difficult to track in flight, and serves, as I have written elsewhere, as the bonded mount of the Scute's own Storm-Lancer Corps. The **Cloud-Ram**, the Panda-Goat to most who keep them, is the primary livestock and textile animal of the Summit-Walkers high in the Engineer's own mountain range. The **Draft-Beetle** serves as a nearly universal transport animal, ranched widely by both Marsupials and Simians across the canopy.

I find the **Dune Dogs** genuinely charming despite their function, which I record here plainly: a hedgehog-meerkat desert insectivore that traps prey passively in fur closer to velcro than to ordinary hair, a trick their own keepers call the Shimmer-Lure, with over a hundred documented regional color variants that I suspect no single archive besides my own has ever tried to fully catalogue. The **Dust-Skipper**, a sand-skimming bird native to the Iron Caldera's borders, protects its eyes with a nictitating sand-shield and glides on feather oil nearly frictionless against the desert's grit. The **Fur-Wyrm** — Peak-Coyote, Tube-Rat, the Slink, depending on the mountain community you ask — is a cold-weather scavenger with an unfortunate reputation for stealing more than rations from careless travelers in the Mountain Range. The **Furnace-Tick**, the Industrial Barnacle to anyone who's lost equipment to one, plagues both the Iron Caldera and the Vaneer Concord, eating machinery rather than flesh, which I have come to think makes it more frightening to an engineer than almost any predator would be to a soldier.

**Gem-Shell Beetles** swarm in metallic-shelled clouds hazardous enough to airships that pilots call the phenomenon Living Hail; I want to note, for the sake of an honest record, that one surviving account ties their coloration to a moon name I have already retired elsewhere in this collection as an old, discarded draft, and I have chosen not to repeat that detail here. The **Glass-Scale Sand-Eel**, a chaos-mutated predator near the Iron Caldera, carries bifurcated sex-based biology I have not seen replicated anywhere else in my research, and its mutation traces directly to Tiraton's own earth-fracturing influence on the region. The **Night-Carapace** — Bobcat-Beetle, Clicking Shadow — hunts by ambush through the alpine forests after dark, and is, I should note for any traveler curious enough to ask, also considered a genuine delicacy, dangerous enough to prepare that only a handful of chefs across the continent are trusted with it. The **Razor-Strider**, sometimes called the Obsidian Razor-Strider for the quills of true volcanic glass it carries, stands seven or eight feet at the shoulder and hunts in packs across the Iron Caldera's open desert — I would not recommend meeting one without an escort.

The **Sky-Grazers** drift through the upper air as living weather vanes, and I have come to rely on shepherds who track them as reliably as I rely on my own Jitter-Rig — their behavior serves as a genuine barometer for Chaos Zone activity, though they face what their handlers call a Static Famine during Shadow Week, when the very energy they depend on for sustenance grows scarce along with everything else the eclipse dampens. The **Solar-Worm**, the Sun-Grub or Golden-Fleece to furriers, provides luxury fur and warmth, seasonally trapped rather than farmed outright. The **Somoan Wood-Hen** — Screamer-Hen, Camp-Badger — functions as a living alarm system and pest control in one small, semi-domesticated body, and I note without much surprise that the Eastern Hounds, ever practical, eat them as readily as they employ them. **Terror-Fowl**, rather less gently known as the Murder-Chicken, are ranched by Marsupial communities in the Hanging Roosts, and I have chosen not to ask further questions about how that ranching is conducted.

I want to give the **Litho-Horrors** their own particular note, because I think they are frequently misunderstood as something more mystical than they actually are. They are not magical entities in any sense my own order recognizes. They are microscopic organisms, mutated into massive, mineral-encrusted behemoths by raw chaos-bleed seeping up from the planet's own core — biological extremophiles, however monstrous the result looks to an untrained eye, not chaos given deliberate malicious form. The **Quake-Stalker**, the Ash-Spider or Ground-Breaker, is a burrowing ambush predator that serves, exclusively, as the Eastern Hounds' bonded heavy-cavalry mount — I have never seen one ridden by anyone outside that culture, and I suspect the bond required to manage one safely simply doesn't transfer. The **Thunder-Strider**, the Ash-Strider or Great-Grey, serves more broadly as bonded cavalry and beast of burden across several factions I've documented in this collection.

The **Titan-Aphid**, the Sugar-Tick to those who farm it, is raised by the Formicidae in what they call the Honeydew-Halls. The **Titan-Boa**, still known to most simply as the King-Snake, is farmed by the Mungo in suspended Coil-Yards I have visited once and do not intend to visit again without considerably more preparation than I brought the first time. The **Titan-Roach** — Mountain Hauler, Wall-Crawler, Siege-Bug — serves as heavy cavalry and beast of burden both, hauling tons of steel straight up vertical walls, ridden into battle by armored Avian infantry in a combination I found genuinely startling to watch the one time I witnessed it in the field. And the **Weaver-Cow**, the Velvet-Spider to anyone unfamiliar with its true nature, is farmed by the Sloth-Folk in dark, humid Web-Barns, its silk woven into the Iron-Weave Canvas I've written of elsewhere in this collection.

## IV. The Canopy Peoples

I want to close this volume with the five peoples who make the mid-canopy's economy function at all, because I think their work deserves to be understood as a coherent culture rather than five separate footnotes scattered across a botanical index.

The **Vulpines** — the Red Brokers, as they're known across most of the trade routes they dominate — serve as diplomats, lawyers, and spice-barons, cultivating Crimson-Clove from their home at Vulpus-Prime, a city I have found genuinely beautiful, built from suspended pagodas strung with silk lanterns that catch the canopy light in a way I have not managed to describe to my own satisfaction in any of my earlier drafts. The **Tarsiers** — the Ghost-Eyes, or Spectral Watchers to those less familiar with them — work as gunsmiths, snipers, and auditors from Eye-Reach, a network of observation towers positioned deliberately over the continent's main trade routes, missing very little that passes beneath them. The **Ranidae** — Frog-Folk, called the Alchemists or the Rain-Callers depending on the region — serve as apothecaries, poisoners, and chemical engineers, backed by a dedicated military arm, the Tox-Troopers, operating from the steam-shrouded bio-domes of Tox-Hollow.

The **Simians** run as high-speed traders from the Wind-Tunnels, cultivating Whisper-Berry alongside a considerable share of the canopy's other produce, while the **Sciurini** — the Gliders, more commonly — serve as the canopy's couriers, cultivating Ghost-Teak from their home in the Crown-Layer, moving goods and messages through the high canopy faster than almost any other people on this continent can manage. Together, these five peoples form the actual working economy beneath every grander nation's trade figures — the ones who grow, gather, guard, and carry the goods that every larger power eventually claims credit for. I have come, over the course of this research, to think they deserve rather more credit than they generally receive.

*— C.*
