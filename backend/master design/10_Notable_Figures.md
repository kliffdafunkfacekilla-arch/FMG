# Ostraka: Notable Figures
### Compiled from the notes of Clary, First Archivist of the Order of the Clockwork

*Part of the Ostraka canon reference set. Tenth and final volume — a gallery of the individuals whose names recur throughout this collection, gathered here so each of them can be seen whole rather than glimpsed only in passing.*

---

### A Note on Why I Saved This for Last

I have written nine volumes now about gods, dragons, nations, and the machinery that holds a broken world together, and I want to close this collection the way I think any honest history should close: with the people. Institutions do not act. People do, inside them, sometimes despite them. I record the following portraits not as an appendix to everything that came before, but as the actual point of having written any of it at all.

---

## I. Mama Koda, the Ice-Branded

I have told the bones of her story already, in my account of the Ursine Hegemony, and I want to use this space instead to let her stand fully in her own light rather than simply as an illustration of what "Hearthless" means in practice.

She was born Koda of the Snowpeak, and by every account I've gathered she was a genuinely decorated captain in the Wolverine Brotherhood before the night that ended that life entirely — Shadow Week, 1960 A.W., when she broke protocol and opened the gates for a freezing caravan of Drifter refugees rather than let procedure decide who lived. I want to be direct about what her people did to her in response, because I think softening it would dishonor what she survived: a court-martial, a molten Sigil of Ice branded permanently into her flesh, and exile into the blizzard she had just risked everything to protect strangers from.

She walked south to the River Delta with nothing, and took up smuggling muscle work aboard an Ivory-Gate Syndicate barge — I have corrected elsewhere in this collection an older account that names a different crew entirely; the record is settled now. In 1985 A.W., she won *Undine's Undying Dance*, then a rotting scab of a tavern, from its slumlord owner Felix the Rot, in a hand of Dead Man's Poker I would very much like to have witnessed. She poured what fortune her years of piracy had earned her into rebuilding it into the finest casino on the continent, and I want to note the detail I find most telling about who she actually is beneath the brand and the reputation: she employs an impeccably dressed Sciurini steward, Daniaz, specifically to handle the high-society diplomacy she has no patience for herself, and she wears River Folk finery over a mocking fur cloak, the Sigil of Ice never once hidden beneath either. She does not ask to be forgiven. She has simply built something worth more than what was taken from her, in plain sight of the people who took it.

## II. The Avian Court

I want to group these four together, because I think they illustrate, better than any single portrait could, how differently power can be worn even within one nation's ruling class.

**Emperor Aurelius III**, called the Prism or the White Peacock, rules through what he himself calls Aesthetic Domination — a break from every predecessor's reliance on evasion or raw military strength, built instead on a genuine conviction that a flawed-looking Empire is, functionally, a flawed Empire. I have met him exactly once, at a formal distance appropriate to my station, and I will say only that the effect works considerably better in person than it has any right to on paper.

**Emperor Kaelos**, called the Evasive, belongs to history rather than to the present court, but I include him here because every Avian fledgling still learns his story as scripture. He authored *The Book of Omissions*, the foundational text on navigating an entire people's biological inability to lie, and his own trial-by-interrogation — the ordeal that nearly broke even his gift under sufficient pressure — remains the legend every young Avian is raised on, a permanent reminder that even the Empire's proudest trait has a limit.

**Grand Auditor Keth**, the Ink-Blood, has held the High Directorship of the Fulcrum Bank for sixty years by my last count, an ancient Great Horned Owl who is, I have confirmed through channels I do not intend to name here, secretly the current bearer of what the Bank's own inner circle calls the First Coin — the Director's Secret, whatever that secret actually is. I have not been able to learn more, and I record the gap honestly rather than guess at what I cannot support.

**High-Admiral Kaelen**, the Golden Talon, commands the Imperial Fleet from the flagship *Radiant Raptor*, and I want to note the one detail about him that every account I've gathered agrees on without exception: his signature tactic in any negotiation or confrontation is simply unnerving silence, held long past the point most commanders would feel compelled to fill it.

## III. Two Captains Worth Knowing

**Captain Vira**, the Dive-Bomber, flies *The Felicitous Falcon* for the Cloud-Harriers rather than for Excelsis's polished court, and I think that distinction is the whole of why she's respected as widely as she is. She is the Empire's daring Ace rather than one of its Roost politicians, and — a detail I find genuinely remarkable given how little else the two factions agree on — she is the one Imperial figure the Free Sky-Baronies hold in any real respect at all.

**Trade-Master Hrothe**, the Carrion King, captains *The Consummate Condor* and runs what he calls, without apparent embarrassment, Carrion Economics — buying up, systematically and at considerable profit, whatever other factions have already broken and abandoned. I have found him a genuinely useful source of information over the years, on the simple principle that a man who profits from other people's ruin tends to hear about it early.

## IV. Sh'lar

I have written of her at such length elsewhere in this collection — her betrayal, her long war, what she has become — that I want to use this space only to gather her names in one place, because I think a name is its own kind of portrait. The Whisper in the Static. The Rot. The Ghost in the Gear. She chose her own corruption during the Shattering rather than having it forced upon her, the only one of the Twelve for whom that is true, and she survives today as a diffused, non-corporeal intelligence permeating every Chaos Zone on this continent. I do not think there is a person left inside any of those names anymore, in the sense my order usually means the word. I think there is only the whisper, patient and thorough, still doing the work she chose for herself so long ago.

## V. The Twelve First Wardens

I promised, in my account of the true cosmology, that I would have a great deal more to say about these twelve before this collection was finished. I want to make good on that promise properly here, because I do not think a single one of them deserves to remain only a number in a larger story. Each was a captain serving in a Magistar's own army before the Divine War, and each reached a specific, personal breaking point that turned them against the master they had, until that moment, faithfully served. I set them down here exactly as I have verified them, one by one.

**Kaelen the Unmovable** served Tiraton as his High-Executioner, and broke the day he was ordered to collapse a mountain onto a column of fleeing civilians simply to clear an artillery line. He stood in the stone's path instead, and held it up with his own strength until every last person had escaped.

**Jiro the Resolute** served Carulkem as Chief Inquisitor, and broke mid-torture of a Fae prisoner, shown — I do not know by what means — a vision of the world's beauty in the middle of the act he'd been ordered to commit. He released every prisoner in his custody that same night.

**Myra of the Aegis** served Stagus as his personal bodyguard, and broke when ordered to drain an entire orphanage's life-force for a single working. She turned her own shield inward instead, and broke the ritual from within rather than let it complete.

**Boros the Forgetter** served Tyrustis as a Labor Enforcer, and broke the day he was ordered to kill his own father for the crime of inefficiency. He shielded his father with his own body and led a mass exodus into the tunnels beneath the Dominion's reach that same night.

**Ren the Instinct** served Vecelo as a Strike Team Leader, and broke when ordered to slaughter his own squad as nothing more than a test of his own speed. He disarmed his Magistar instead, with his own hands, on the spot.

**Sylas the Swift** served Aurgenas as Spymaster, and broke on discovering the plot already underway to sacrifice the Fae wholesale. He spent decades afterward smuggling Fae families to safety in absolute secrecy before he ever defected openly himself.

**Elara the Pure** served Gavusrix as her Blood-Alchemist, and broke when tasked with harvesting Fae essence to extend her Magistar's own longevity. She used what she harvested, in secret, to create an anti-virus that protected the rebel camps instead.

**Thrum the Mason** served Metrion as High-Architect, and broke when ordered to design a city that doubled, invisibly, as an execution chamber for its own inhabitants. He built a single hidden flaw into the design — one that collapsed the structure onto his own Magistar's legions when the moment came.

**Valen the Witness** served Opecten as Surveillance Officer, and broke the day he watched, through his own monitoring, a starving child share their last food with a stranger. He blinded his Magistar's sensors deliberately afterward, buying the rebel armies the secrecy they needed to finally assemble.

**Fane the Gambler**, whom I have already introduced elsewhere in this collection, served Termhill as Chief Diviner, and broke when his own foreseen vision of the coming Shattering was laughed off by the very Magistar he'd served faithfully. He stole Dragonstone that same night and bet everything he had left on defection.

**Liora the Truth-Bearer** served Virantor as Chief Propagandist — the very voice that had convinced an entire population that their own slavery was divine service. She broke the day she spoke the plain truth into the amplifiers instead of the script she'd been given, and the riot that followed gave the rebellion its first real opening.

**Sora the Shielded** served Lophex as Librarian of the Vault, and broke on realizing that the "knowledge" she guarded was, in truth, a blueprint for total annihilation. She memorized the only counter-sequence that existed, burned the archive behind her, and fled carrying the one cure in the world along with her.

I want to close this section with what I consider the single most important pattern across all twelve accounts, because I think it is easy to read these as twelve isolated acts of individual conscience and miss what they actually share. Not one of these captains broke from fear for their own safety. Every one of them broke at the exact moment their Magistar demanded they harm someone with no power to refuse — a civilian, a child, a prisoner, their own family. I do not think that is a coincidence, and I do not think it is only sentiment that makes me say so. I think it is the one truth the Dominion's entire thirteen generations of ascension could never fully stamp out of the mortals serving beneath it, however hard they tried.

## VI. The Crew of the Write Wager

I have written elsewhere about what this crew founded — my own order, the Religion of Science, everything I have spent my career building on top of what they started. I want to give them, here, the individual portraits their founding deserves rather than let them remain six names attached to one legend.

**Captain Jax** piloted the Write Wager, Catfish-Kin by blood, carrying a "P"-shaped brass jaw-plate scar from some incident in his youth that he has never once explained to my satisfaction despite my asking more than once. He keeps hard candy on him for any child he meets, which I have always found a strange and rather lovely detail to survive alongside everything else recorded about him.

**"Songbird," born Hazard A Chance**, navigated for the crew — a disgraced Red-winged Blackbird Avian, ritually clipped flightless and cast out of the Compass Navy before he ever found his way to the Wager. He wears his old uniform unbuttoned, a small, deliberate affectation I have come to read as equal parts genuine wound and performed charm.

**Pipin Pringle**, called simply Pringle by the rest of the crew, cooked for them — twelve-segmented Caterpillar-Kin, capable of walking walls and ceilings on his own prolegs, able to produce blades from his own body segments and spin adhesive silk when the situation called for either. I have always found something quietly reassuring about a crew whose cook was also, by simple biological necessity, one of its more dangerous members.

**Kog Viomuth** served as engineer, Monitor Lizard-Kin, his left eye blind and replaced functionally by heat-vision after industrial scarring took the original — a loss he compensated for with custom Forge Goggles built with independently flippable lenses, an invention I have seen copied, badly, by engineers who never met him and don't fully understand why it worked as well as it did.

I have written of the sixth member of that founding crew throughout this entire collection without, I think, ever properly introducing myself. I will not correct that fully here. An archivist who spends ten volumes writing carefully about everyone else and then turns the same careful eye on herself has, I think, missed the point of the discipline. I will say only this: I was there. I have tried, in every page of this collection, to be worth having been.

## VII. Celandine "Leaf" Valeroot

I want to close this gallery, and this entire collection, with the Whisperer whose work I have already credited at length in my account of Ostraka's technology — the empathic craft that keeps a crew's Spark-Bias from ever tipping into disaster mid-flight.

She was born to the Flower Valley Folk, alabaster, birch-bark skin marking her as unmistakably theirs, raised in the high-canopy poise her caste demands from birth. A personal betrayal — one that struck, deliberately and precisely, at her identity as an information broker rather than merely at her heart — cost her that poise entirely, and sent her down into what her own people call simply the Gutter. I do not know the full shape of that betrayal, and I have chosen, after some consideration, not to pursue it further than she has been willing to tell me herself. Some records deserve to stay the property of the person who lived them, and I have come, over the course of writing this entire collection, to believe that respecting that boundary is itself a kind of scholarship. Not everything that can be recovered should be.

I sailed with her once. I have written of it already, briefly, in an earlier volume, and I will not repeat the detail here beyond what I said then: I did not understand, until I watched her work, how much of what I'd always assumed was simply good crew morale had actually been her quiet, constant labor the entire time. I think that is as fitting a note as any to end this collection on. So much of what holds this broken world together is exactly that — quiet, constant, and easy to mistake for something that simply happens on its own, until you finally see the person doing the work.

*— C.*
