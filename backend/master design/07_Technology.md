# Ostraka: Technology
### Compiled from the notes of Clary, First Archivist of the Order of the Clockwork

*Part of the Ostraka canon reference set. Seventh volume, covering the substance that everything else in this collection ultimately runs on.*

---

### A Note on Writing About Machines

I did not expect, when I began this work, that the volume on technology would be the one where I felt closest to the people I was writing about. But machines, on Ostraka, are never simply machines. Every engine, every coin, every navigational instrument in this account is built from the same substance that shattered the world in the first place, harnessed by people who mostly understand it only well enough to survive it, not well enough to fully control it. I have come to think that is the most honest thing I can say about our entire civilization: we did not solve the chaos that broke us. We learned, generation by generation, to burn a small, carefully measured portion of it for light and warmth, and we have called that progress ever since.

---

## I. Dragonstone

Everything begins with Dragonstone, and I want to correct, first, an error I have seen repeated in tech-focused writing across half the continent — including, I will admit, in earlier drafts of my own notes. Dragonstone is not "a crystallized fragment of the Celestial Clockwork," whatever the older engineering manuals still insist. I have documented, at considerable length elsewhere in this collection, what it actually is: crystallized moon-fall, meteoric debris from Erranith's shattered body, raining down once roughly every 169 years and gathered — feverishly, competitively — until the next storm comes.

It is not only cosmic in origin, though. Massive deposits pulse deep underground at the core of each of the twelve Dragon Prisons, which makes a grim kind of sense once you remember, as I have written elsewhere, that those prisons are themselves built from Cruorbus's shards — the substance was never entirely gone from the world, only redistributed by the same catastrophe that scattered it in the sky. The River Folk, more practically, have long collected what they call river rock Dragonstone, polished fragments washed down from mountain peaks by ordinary waterfalls, no expedition required. I mention this because I think it is easy, writing about something this cosmically significant, to forget that ordinary people gather it the way anyone gathers a useful stone from a riverbed — carefully, but without much ceremony.

## II. The Economy Built on It

Raw Dragonstone is ground into what artisans call Dusted Ink and painted as sigils to channel its chaos energy — this is the actual mechanical basis for every piece of Aether-technology on Ostraka, and the complexity of a given sigil determines the tier of technology it can support. Powdered Dragonstone, D-Dust, became the continent's common-use currency for exactly the reasons you'd expect: portable, valuable, and directly useful to anyone with the skill to work it. It also proved fatally easy to counterfeit. Blackstone — ordinary rock temporarily charged with Aura to fool a merchant's eye and a basic detection charm alike — crippled large-scale trade for a period I have found genuinely difficult to overstate the cost of, and threatened, by more than one account I trust, real geopolitical instability before anyone found a lasting answer.

The Avian Empire found that answer, and I want to record plainly that it is the true foundation of their financial dominance, more than any diplomatic cleverness the Empire likes to credit itself with in its own official histories. The Aetherium Coin, forged specifically during the total darkness of Shadow Week to be functionally uncounterfeitable, became the standard the rest of the continent had no real choice but to adopt. The Fulcrum Bank functions today as the de facto central bank for every major power on Ostraka, and between that position and their majority, carefully undisclosed ownership of the Gilded Compass Cartel, the Avians hold the ability to economically strangle a rival faction without ever raising a weapon against them. I have come to think of this as their real Global Watchmen leverage — not the goodwill their public reputation trades on, but simple, patient control of everyone else's currency.

## III. Why the Sky Mattered

Before the Aether-Skiff, I want to be direct about what Ostraka actually was: twelve isolated landmasses, functionally cut off from one another by impassable Chaos Zones, with only rare and dangerous ocean routes offering any way around them. The Avians' natural gift of flight made them the first people on this continent capable of bypassing that isolation entirely, and for a long stretch of history, the only people who could. I do not think it is an accident that they became the world's central hub for trade and communication as a result, nor that the Aether-Skiff's invention date — 1500 A.W. by every record I trust — marks the beginning of an entire named era, the Combustion of the Skies. A single piece of technology rewrote the shape of the world's politics more thoroughly than any war I have documented in this collection.

The engineering underneath every skiff is what my order calls the Closed-Loop Aether-Drive, or, in the older and more evocative phrasing I've come to prefer, the Centrifuge of Creation. Raw Dragonstone bleeds unaligned chaos continuously; the drive filters and channels that bleed into usable silent lift, propulsion, industrial heat, and electricity, all from the same underlying source. I want to note, because I think it is easy to lose sight of in a purely mechanical description, that this is the literal engineering behind the phrase "civilization runs on Dragonstone" — not a metaphor, but an accurate description of what happens inside every airship's hull on every flight taken across this continent.

I should say, too, that I do not consider Aether-Tech simply mechanics dressed up in unfamiliar vocabulary. Every serious practitioner I have interviewed describes it as something closer to alchemy — high-stakes, genuinely dangerous alchemy, since what they are working with is born from the same chaos that shattered this world in the first place. I have come to agree with them. Controlling that substance is not a solved problem anywhere on Ostraka. It is, at best, a paradox we have gotten reasonably skilled at surviving.

## IV. Two Materials Worth Knowing

I want to record two further materials that recur constantly across this collection, because I think a reader who encounters them elsewhere deserves to understand where they actually come from.

Silk-Steel, sometimes called Iron-Weave, is Ostraka's highest-tensile-strength material, underpinning heavy industrial, architectural, and naval construction across every nation wealthy enough to afford it. It is produced under a strict Bradypod monopoly — the same slow, careful people I have documented at both the Avian Empire's Fulcrum Bank and among the Flower Valley Folk's Slow-Vault, a detail I find fitting given how patient the production process itself is said to be.

Aether-Clear, the Quill-Kin's genuine medicinal export, I can now confirm the recipe for after considerable cross-referencing: fermented and distilled Ozone-Milk, the Cloud-Harriers' own export, combined with a second stabilizing ingredient — Lunar Ghost-Blooms, harvested at real risk by the Dust-Husk Riders. It treats what practitioners call Corruption Sickness, the lingering harm of Chaos Zone exposure, and I want to note the quiet cleverness of how it moves through the world undetected: the Quill-Kin are, to every outside eye, simply the continent's apothecaries. Nobody questions a bulk herb shipment arriving at an apothecary's door. The Ghost Flowers hide in plain sight, inside the one profession no one thinks to search.

## V. The Blowout

I have written about this elsewhere in this collection, but I think it deserves its own clear entry here, separate from any single faction's story, because it is the one danger every piece of Aether-Tech on this continent shares in common. A Blowout is not an explosion, whatever the word might suggest to someone unfamiliar with it. It is an unmaking — instantaneous biological petrification, localized gravity reversed without warning, matter simply ceasing to be mid-motion. It happens whenever a Grove's seal, or a piece of Aether-Tech built on the same underlying principles, fails and lets raw, unrouted chaos flow through completely unfiltered. I have seen the aftermath twice in my career. I have no wish to describe either occasion further than that.

## VI. The Jitter-Rig

Every serious navigator on Ostraka carries one, and I want to correct, here, the same error I corrected earlier in my account of the true cosmology: the instrument does not detect "tectonic plates torquing during a rotational shift," whatever generations of navigational manuals have insisted. There was never a plate to torque. What the Jitter-Rig actually detects — a raw, unsigiled Dragonstone shard suspended in a tuning-fork framework of iron, copper, and quartz — is the leyline network itself re-tensioning as the world's chaos flow shifts course, felt through the instrument's resonance before it can be felt anywhere else. The correction changes what the navigator believes they're sensing. It changes nothing at all about why the instrument matters, or how a skilled hand reads it to adjust course before a flow-shift catches a ship unprepared.

During Shadow Week specifically, a navigator must manually adapt the flow through the rig to prevent an engine desync, since ambient chaos behaves differently while the whole network runs slowed by the eclipse. I want to note that this still holds entirely true under the corrected cosmology — the specific danger has simply changed character, from a flow "violently tearing" to one merely slowed and calmed, but the Jitter-Rig's actual job never changes: keeping a ship's systems synced to whatever the ambient flow happens to be doing at that particular moment, regardless of which explanation for it turns out to be true.

## VII. Spark-Bias and the Whisperer's Craft

I want to close this volume with the one piece of Aether-Tech that has nothing to do with metal or fire at all, and everything to do with the crew standing inside the hull.

Because magic on Ostraka is, at its root, a biological byproduct of chaos, a crew's own emotional state can genuinely destabilize the reaction inside a ship's Aetheric coils — what practitioners call Spark-Bias. A Whisperer, typically drawn from the Flower Valley Folk and trained in a kind of empathic social awareness I have never fully managed to describe to my own satisfaction, harmonizes a crew's collective emotional output to keep that reaction from spiraling into feedback and desyncing the ship's systems entirely. I have sailed with one exactly once, on a voyage I would rather not detail further here, and I will say only this: I did not understand, until I watched her work, how much of what I'd always assumed was simple good crew morale had actually been her quiet, constant labor the entire time.
