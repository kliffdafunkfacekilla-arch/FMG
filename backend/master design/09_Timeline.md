# Ostraka: Timeline
### Compiled from the notes of Clary, First Archivist of the Order of the Clockwork

*Part of the Ostraka canon reference set. Ninth volume, laying the whole of recorded history against a single line, era by era.*

---

### A Note on Dating a Broken World

I want to say plainly, before this volume begins, that dating anything on Ostraka is harder than it sounds, and I do not think most historians outside my own order appreciate quite how hard. A calendar assumes a world that holds still long enough to count consistently against, and ours, as I have written at length elsewhere in this collection, does not. What follows is the best reconstruction my order has managed, cross-checked wherever possible against the founding stories of the factions I've documented throughout this collection. I found, to my genuine relief, that everything lines up cleanly once the dates are properly reconciled. I do not take that agreement for granted. It took considerable work to arrive at.

---

## I. Before the Wardens

Two eras predate the calendar most of Ostraka actually uses, and I include them here mostly for the sake of completeness, since almost nothing dated survives from either one.

**Era B.S.**, "Before Shattering," runs up to roughly 6650 B.S. by the reckoning my order has settled on. **Era S.S.**, "The Shattered Sky," follows from that point to roughly 3000 B.S. I want to note something about the name here that I think matters, because I have seen it misread more than once by researchers newer to this material than I am. "Shattering" in this context is not a description of the ground itself breaking apart — I have spent an entire separate volume in this collection correcting exactly that misunderstanding. It is simply the name history gave to the cataclysm of the Fae Exodus and the Magistars' ascension, the event, not a physical description of the world afterward. The name survives because it is evocative, not because it is literal, and I have chosen to keep using it for that reason rather than replace it with something more accurate but less alive on the page.

## II. The A.W. Calendar

Everything else in this volume is dated **A.W.** — "After Wardens" — counting forward from the era I have documented at length elsewhere in this collection: the twelve traitor captains who exposed Sh'lar's deception during the Divine War, not defectors from the Magistars' ascension itself, a distinction I want to restate here because I have seen the two confused even by researchers who should know better.

**Era I** covers the years 0 through 150 A.W., and I record honestly that no dated event from this earliest period has survived clearly enough for me to include here. It was, by every account I can piece together, a quiet founding period — the world stabilizing, slowly, under the Patch the Engineer left behind.

**Era II**, the Feral Epoch, runs from 151 to 500 A.W., and carries two events I have documented at length in earlier volumes of this collection. The Schism of the Scar, roughly 250 A.W., split one united botanical people into the Sylvan Empire and the Flower Valley Folk — I have told that story properly elsewhere and will not repeat it here beyond the date itself. The Delta Wars, roughly 400 A.W., founded the divide between the River Folk and the Sump-Kin that persists to this day.

**Era III**, the Era of the Widening Eye, runs from 501 to 800 A.W. — and I want to note, because I did not understand this myself until relatively late in my research, that the name is not poetic license. It describes, quite literally, the psychic shock an entire generation of newly sentient animal-folk experienced upon first perceiving the true, terrifying tension holding this world together, an event I've written of at greater length in my account of the Wardens. The Webweaver Wars and the Gruesome Accords that ended them span from roughly 151 through 700 A.W., founding the Vaneer Concord's governance as I've documented it in an earlier volume — a war that overlaps Era II's boundary considerably, which I record without concern, since wars have never respected the tidy lines historians draw around them afterward.

**Era IV**, the Age of First Contact, runs from 801 to 1499 A.W. Two events anchor it in my own research. The Magistar engine core explosion at the onset of a particularly violent Shadow Week, roughly 1000 A.W., founded the Avian Empire's Doctrine of the Mint. The Opal-Wallow Summit, roughly 1080 A.W., founded the Fulcrum Bank's proxy system, a piece of financial architecture I've written about at length in my account of Ostraka's technology and economy.

**Era V**, the Combustion of the Skies, runs from 1500 to 1850 A.W., and I want to record something that gave me real satisfaction to finally confirm: the Aether-Skiff was invented in exactly 1500 A.W., the precise year the era is named for. It was not a slow drift into a new age. It was a single invention, dated cleanly, that reshaped the entire continent's politics within a generation — the trigger for the Ivory-Gate Syndicate's founding conflict, for the pattern of sky-lane invasions that runs through both the Sylvan Empire's and the Flower Valley Folk's history, and for most of the airship-era faction origins I've documented throughout this collection.

**Era VI**, the Modern Gridlock, runs from 1851 to 2000 A.W., and brings us to the present day, or close enough to it that I record the remaining uncertainty honestly rather than pretend to a precision I don't have. The Guerrilla Clans' violation by a Sky-Baron crew, which I've written of elsewhere as the true origin of the Ghostwind Raiders, dates to 1892 A.W. Mama Koda's rise as a pirate captain dates to 1985 A.W., and her exile — the event that made her rise possible in the first place — is confirmed at 1960 A.W.

## III. Where We Actually Stand

I want to be honest that the present day has never been stated as a hard number anywhere in my order's records, and I do not think that absence is an oversight. Mama Koda's exile was explicitly re-dated, in the more recent and more authoritative version of this timeline, specifically — the phrase I recovered was "to keep her an active, living threat in the present day." I take that as confirmation that we sit somewhere in the back half of Era VI, likely close to but before 2000 A.W., without a precise year my order has ever committed to paper. I record the gap honestly. I do not think it needs closing artificially.

I want to flag, too, a superseded draft I recovered early in my research and initially mistook for current fact. An earlier version of this same timeline used entirely different numbers — a Last Reckoning at 1590 A.W., Koda's exile at 1620 A.W., a stated current year of 1650 A.W. My order's own records mark the six-era, 1960 A.W. version as the later and more accurate revision, and I have treated it as canon throughout this collection accordingly. I record the older numbers here only so a future researcher who stumbles across them elsewhere understands they've found a discarded draft, not a contradiction I failed to notice.

Three factions I have documented at length in earlier volumes — the Scute Confederacy, the Iron Caldera, and the Ivory-Gate Syndicate — all carry rich founding stories with no fixed year attached to any of them. I do not consider this a gap worth losing sleep over. Not every history needs a date to be true. I record it here only so a future archivist, more patient than I've had time to be, knows exactly where to look if they'd like to try pinning one down themselves.

*— C.*
