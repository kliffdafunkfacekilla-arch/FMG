# 4.13 The Eastern Hounds (Ash-Walkers) of the Howling Steppes

**Patron Dragon:** Termhill, The Black Star (Omen) — already resolved in Section 2 (Korus was folded into Termhill as their regional epithet). A dedicated expos� on the Hounds uses **"Ghal"** as the name of their psychic tormentor throughout — same deprecated-placeholder pattern as Kael, Zylos, Valkor, and Igni elsewhere (it's on that same confirmed-deprecated list). Reading "Ghal" as Termhill throughout the material below; the substance is consistent with Omen's temporal-bleed domain regardless of the name used.

**Defining phenomenon — The Haunting:** the Howling Steppes are a zone where "the temporal gears of the Celestial Clockwork have slipped their cogs" — a localized, permanent temporal-bleed failure, exactly what Omen/Termhill's domain would produce at its worst. Survival requires abandoning long-term planning and memory itself, since memory becomes a liability that anchors the mind to traumatic echoes. Their culture is built around strategic forgetting rather than resilience through remembering — a sharp contrast to the Ursine's Hearth-Culture or the Scute's vengeance-memory, worth keeping as deliberate thematic variety across factions living next to different dragons.

**The Primacy of Instinct:** generations of psychic assault taught the Hounds "never trust your eyes alone" — an acute reliance on other senses and raw instinct over sight, since Termhill's influence works through illusion and phantoms of the past. **The Fortress of the Spirit:** communal chanting, droning, and drums aren't entertainment — they're a deliberate psycho-acoustic bulwark meant to "drown out the whispers" and fortify the pack's collective mind.

**The Grave-Touched — shaman selection:** a genuine paradox in Hound culture: they fear the past yet maintain a deeply shamanistic ancestor-connection, resolved by treating respect for the dead as active defense (an improperly buried spirit becomes a tool Termhill can use to haunt the living — "letting your grand-dad haunt the halls" is one of the gravest possible insults). When a cub is recognized as "grave-touched," the current shaman leaves them alone at night at the start of **Shadow Week** to face an unknown ordeal, sometimes for up to a year, returning as a fully-realized shaman. This lines up precisely with the corrected Shadow Week mechanic (Section 1): it's exactly when Termhill's containment weakens and his influence bleeds through strongest — the ordeal is a real confrontation with the dragon's power, not just tradition.

**The Roaring Fire:** their annual gathering — every nomadic pack converging at one site for enormous bonfires, drumming, and chanting visible for miles — is a deliberate act of collective defiance, a yearly declaration that the Hounds are still here despite the Haunting.
