# 4.19 The River Ghosts (the "Subterranean Exiles")

**Not supernatural — a hidden society.** What surface-dwellers call "ghosts" haunting the waterways are actually an exiled sub-culture of River Folk, biological pariahs driven underground generations ago, now living along a hidden subterranean highway of warm mineral caves connecting the terraces of the Hive Commonwealth to the swamps of the Sump-Kin — an uncontrolled logistical corridor no faction currently governs.

**Culture — total inversion:** cut off from the surface for generations, their oral tradition treats surface-dwellers as the true monsters — a sleepless Vaneer Logic-Master or an Avian obsessed with an abstract concept like "Truth" reads to them as an incomprehensible horror, not the other way around. When they must surface, they adopt a "beggar-merchant" disguise, likely using low-level Biomancy or Illusion to mask their pale, semi-aquatic features.

**The Haunting Doctrine — two-tiered defense:** their survival strategy is a deliberate, weaponized myth, not random supernatural activity. Tier one is non-lethal psychological theater (boiling springs, disorienting echoes, a pale shape vanishing underwater) meant to scare off the superstitious. Tier two is escalation: an investigator who won't be deterred is eliminated quietly, the body arranged to look like an accident or animal attack, letting the region's genuine natural dangers take the blame.

**The unraveling — an active plot thread:** their myth-based defense has survived for generations against superstition, but is now failing against something new — the **Order of the Clockwork** (Section 4.22) has identified their travel patterns as a data anomaly rather than folklore, cross-referencing shipping manifests and traveler reports to correctly map the "haunting" as a real transit corridor. The Order doesn't believe in ghosts; it believes in patterns. A confrontation between the Exiles' world of myth and the Order's world of data is treated as inevitable — a live, unresolved tension rather than settled history.
