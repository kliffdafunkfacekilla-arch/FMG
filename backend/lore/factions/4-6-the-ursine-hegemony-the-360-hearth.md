# 4.6 The Ursine Hegemony — The 360-Hearth

**Patron Dragon:** Stagus, The Unyielding Glazier (Ordo) — matches "Frozen Heart" cult (Titanium dust ingestion, targets Ursine communities specifically).

**Culture:** Built around **Hearth-Culture** — isolation is treated as a literal thermal/survival threat. The "Mama Bear" doctrine: any threat to "the Cubs" (the vulnerable) is met with absolute violence. Fits Stagus's Endurance/Structure domain well — a culture organized entirely around collective, structural resilience. Confirmed directly: the **Treaty of the Hearth**, "written in blood and snow," is their founding document — independent northern tribes unifying to break an industrialized invasion. A "Reluctant Hegemony": dominant not from ambition but from a shared, multi-species philosophy of contentment ("The Bare Necessities") that turns ferociously effective the moment the collective "den" is threatened.

**Population:** a genuine multi-species Hearth-Culture, not just Ursine alone — the Ursine (Bear-Folk) embody the core values of patience and protective endurance; the Big Cats (Lynx, Lions, Panthers, Snow Tigers) are fully integrated and prize "the art of surprise" as a respected skill; the Owls are the scholarly leadership, running the Hegemony's own renowned library/university dedicated to practical science over esoteric theory. All evolved in close proximity to the Convergence, which is precisely why mutual support became existential rather than optional.

**Governance — Hearth-Dukes and the Thermal Grapple:** a working-class feudalism. Every community answers to a Hearth-Duke, appointed by a High-Matriarch to maintain local industry and the communal hearth — a Duke who hoards heat or mismanages resources can be unseated by the community itself. Disputes at any level, even against a Duke, are settled by **the Thermal Grapple**: an unarmed, hours-long wrestling match overseen by four neutral witnesses, adapted to the local biome to forge respect through shared physical trial.

**The Ember Keepers:** the Hegemony's magic-user order — Ursine culture treats magic itself as a kind of creeping decay that must be yoked into community service, so Spark-bearers are drafted into thermal-regulation duty rather than left to their own devices.

**Founding — the Siege of Snowpeak, confirmed:** the invasion that "wrote the Treaty in blood and snow" identified — it was the **Iron Caldera**. Following their Age of Wings (~1520 A.W.), the Ironborn desperately needed fireproof Stone-Root lumber to fuel their new Aether-drives. They marched heavy Stone-Scale infantry to the boiling Anvil-Run River and built a heat-shielded Tungsten pontoon bridge straight through the scalding steam — a genuinely terrifying industrial invasion of Ursine territory. It forced the isolated northern tribes to unite: Feline outriders bled the supply lines in the steam-valleys, the Wolverine Brotherhood mired siege engines in the Timber-March, and the Ursine Hearthguard finally broke the invasion against the cliffs of the High Den. The Owls brokered the eventual peace, forging the modern Hegemony. *(Worth noting: this complicates the Scute's "philosophical mirror, mutual respect" read on the Iron Caldera from Section 4.5 — the Ironborn have a real invasion in their past against a neighboring people, not just a peaceful shared ethos of endurance. Both can be true; it just means the Ironborn's reputation depends heavily on who's telling the story.)*

**Justice — "The Law of the Hearth":** minor offenses get practical, direct restitution (an offender re-thatching the roof they damaged); no prisons exist, since locking someone away would weaken the collective. The one true horror of Ursine society, for major offenses, is being declared **"Hearthless"** — not execution, but the erasure of identity itself. For a culture entirely defined by collective warmth, exile is treated as a fate worse than death, carried out as a solemn public ritual.

**Shadow Week — the Hearthguard's Vigil:** their core principle (order against chaos) reaches its yearly apex during Shadow Week. It doubles as a rite of passage for the young: groups of younglings, under a veteran Vigil Master, are given charge of a single signal hearth through the total darkness. The real test isn't keeping the fire lit — it's rebuilding the community's trust through diligence when it inevitably gets hard, which is what actually shapes a youngling's character.

**The Wolverine Brotherhood ("We serve the Duty, not the Man")** — an independent martial order, not a national army: apolitical, incapable of treachery by reputation, and undefeated as guards ("no one has gotten past them to date that is known of"). They serve as elite guards for both the Ursine Hegemony and the Heartland Alliance while answering to neither — a rare cross-faction institution trusted by two governments that otherwise don't share much.
