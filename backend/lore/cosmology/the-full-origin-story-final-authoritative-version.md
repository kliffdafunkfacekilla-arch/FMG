# The full origin story (final, authoritative version)

**The Divine Age.** In the beginning, the gods reproduced and spread across the universe, filling it with stars.

**The Birth of the Fae** was a wonder in itself. A local god bound himself to his own star so his people would always have a sun — becoming the Sun itself, eternal and undying. In doing so he left two things behind: his eye became **Erranith**, and his own discarded remains became a rogue planet, **Cruorbus** ("The Blood Orphan") — both inert at first, not yet in any orbit.

**The Fae lived in harmony for an era**, befriending the other gods of the cosmos — the beings who would later be called **the Elder Gods** were already known to them, not strangers.

**Man appears.** The Fae took on the role of mentors to the emerging mortal races — "the 12 types of man" — guiding them, until those mortals grew hasty and greedy for what the Fae possessed.

**The Fae sealed themselves away.** Rather than let mankind seize their magic, the Fae used their power to seal *themselves* inside the twelve Sacred Groves, withholding their knowledge from man entirely. *(This is the Groves' original purpose — Fae sanctuary-vaults, long before they became Dragon Prisons. The same containment magic that made them safe havens is exactly what made them viable prisons later.)*

**Man went to war.** Twelve warlords rose to dominance, and on the brink of a final battle that would have destroyed them all, they parleyed instead: join forces, and turn their combined might on the Fae rather than on each other.

**The Dominion ruled for thirteen generations,** growing in power through blood magic — sacrifice, body-hosting, and spirit stasis to extend their rule across generations. In that time they harnessed the whole planet and, monstrously, warped the twelve types of man into twisted, inhuman forms.

**The Fae barriers finally weakened.** Facing defeat, the Fae made a desperate concession: they agreed to help the Dominion and share their knowledge rather than be conquered outright.

**Sh'lar was turned.** Soon after this alliance began, Sh'lar — a Fae representative — was seduced by the Dominion's blood magic and betrayed her own people, revealing the hidden 13th Power (the School of Primordial Chaos) underlying the Twelve. Fae sacrifices were attempted. In response, the Fae performed **the Exodus** — fleeing through a portal to the Barrens, tearing open the Weeping Wound in the process.

**The Exodus drew the attention of the Elder Gods.** At first, tricked — likely by Sh'lar's influence — the gods turned on *each other* rather than the true threat. It was **the traitor captains** — mortal soldiers serving in the Dominion's armies, not Magistars themselves — who exposed the deception and redirected the Elder Gods toward the real enemy.

**The last stand.** Sh'lar faked a parley, buying time while the twelve Dominion warlords completed their ascension ritual in secret. **The dragons were born, and immediately began destroying everything.**

**The second sacrifice.** The surviving Elder Gods gave everything to save the world, dissolving their own forms into the twelve biggest pieces of the broken Celestial Clockwork to build the containment system that became the Ley-line Lattice. Sh'lar poisoned even this ritual with her own life, ensuring a permanent leak in the system — the true source of every Blowout and every successful cult since.

**The Engineer, alone**, finished the work his fellow Elder Gods began — physically sealing each dragon inside a shard of Cruorbus's own debris, moving the finished prisons to the Sacred Groves, and weaving the Ley-line Lattice that holds the shattered world together. Before he laid down to die and petrified into the central mountain range, he granted immortality to the traitor captains who'd helped him — **the last surviving humans on Ostraka.**

**Not every religion in Ostraka is a Fae con — just some.** Where a faction's faith is demonstrably tied to a real, present Magistar's Signature Chaos (the Coastal Theocracy's Hum, tied directly to Virantor's actual Aura influence; the Scute's eclipse-tracking divination, reading real chaos-flow patterns) or a real god (the Church of the Eternal Dawn's Sun worship, the Wolves' Moon Cult, the Porcupines' Cruorbus worship), that's genuine, not Fae manipulation. The Fae's actual con isn't inventing new religions — it's infiltrating existing real ones, whispering as a false divine voice within whatever faith a susceptible mind already holds, anywhere they can reach one, to manipulate mortals toward reopening the door to the Barrens.
