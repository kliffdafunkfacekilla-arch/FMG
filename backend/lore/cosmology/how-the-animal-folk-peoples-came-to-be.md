# How the animal-folk peoples came to be
The First Wardens' vigil didn't happen over an empty world. Bathed in the "Divine Echo" radiating from The Engineer's calcified body and saturated by the leaking energy of the Twelve Powers, the wildlife of the twelve primordial biomes underwent an accelerated natural ascent to full sentience — **every animal-folk faction cataloged in Section 4 is the evolved heir of this radiation, not a separate creation.** To make sure the Sacred Spire would never run out of guardians, one of the surviving Wardens (the Prophet) wove automated traps called **the Silent Bell** into the landscape, triggering whenever a newly-sentient animal-kin with the Spark walked over one. The first activations weren't triumphant — the psychic shock of suddenly perceiving the true, terrifying tension holding the world together is exactly what the Timeline (Section 7) calls **"the Era of the Widening Eye" (501–800 A.W.)** — that name is a literal description, not just a label.

The Wardens laid down **the Pilgrim's Path** — hidden totem-marked routes guiding newly awakened Spark-bearers safely toward the Sacred Spire — and this is also, confirmed, the direct origin of **the Mandate of the Chain** (Section 4.20): they found a fragmented cache of this stashed Warden lore and, unable to understand the whole picture, built their entire anti-magic religion on a misreading of it. The dark irony already noted stands confirmed at the highest level of canon: their chain rituals near the Groves genuinely do recharge the prison seals, exactly as the Wardens intended pilgrims' passage to do.

**The Warden civil war, precisely:** Sh'lar's corruption ignited a brutal civil war within the Engineer's petrified remains — nine of the twelve Traitor Captains were slain by their own kind. Because the survivors had used their divine gifts to spill kin-blood, The Engineer's own failsafe triggered and revoked their immortality. The **three survivors — Thrum (post-war title "the Architect"), the Prophet, and Valen (post-war title "the Mage")** — spent their remaining mortal years carving their legacy directly into The Engineer's head and torso, building the **Sacred Spire**: a fortress with wards "carved from god-flesh" that violently reject anyone touched by dragon-corruption or Sh'lar's taint (escalating ringing, crushing pressure, and fatal aneurysm for those who don't flee in time). *(Thrum and Valen are the same individuals as "Thrum the Mason" and "Valen the Witness" in the Twelve First Wardens table above — Mason/Witness were their roles before defecting; Architect/Mage are their roles after founding the Spire. "The Prophet" doesn't have a confirmed match to one of the twelve names yet — flagging as open.)*

**The Grey Wardens today** are structured around the Spire's own trial system: **the Reckoning of the Spark** (initiation), **the Chasm of the Lathes** (first trial, Vault II access), **the Dance of the Searing Shadow** (ascent to becoming one of the Eyeless), and finally **the Tombs of the Petrified Cores** — a deep labyrinth only navigable via Chaos Sight, holding **the Stasis Council**: twelve stone alcoves, one per biome/dragon prison, where the oldest Eyeless elders eventually retreat to rest in a generational relay. **Vault III**, the deepest archive (raw Magistar histories, the Flesh-Lathes' ledgers, the codex of stolen Fae lore), has no mechanical lock at all — it only opens when all twelve alcoves are simultaneously occupied by elders representing every biome, meaning it can stay sealed for centuries if even one animal lineage fails to produce a Warden.
